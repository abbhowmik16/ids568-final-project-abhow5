Chunking is the process of splitting long documents into smaller pieces before embedding and indexing them. Chunking is necessary because embedding models and language models work better with manageable pieces of text than with entire long documents.
Chunk size and overlap are important design choices. If chunks are too small, they may lose context and make retrieval less useful. If chunks are too large, they may contain too much unrelated information and reduce precision. Overlap helps preserve continuity between chunks by repeating a small amount of text from the end of one chunk at the beginning of the next.
Common chunking strategies include fixed-size chunking, sentence-based chunking, and semantic chunking. Fixed-size chunking is simple and reproducible. Sentence-based chunking can preserve meaning better. Semantic chunking attempts to split text at natural topic boundaries.
There is no universal best chunk size. Good practice is to test multiple chunk sizes such as 256, 512, and 1024 tokens or roughly equivalent word counts, then evaluate retrieval quality and answer grounding.
